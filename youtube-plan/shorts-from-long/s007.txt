The power goes out. Your lights die. Your fridge dies. Your Wi-Fi dies. But turn on the tap... and the water still runs. Why?

Because your water isn't pushed by electricity. It's pushed by height. Follow the pipe under your street... and it climbs a hill, to a tank on legs. That's a water tower.

The water up there is heavy. Its own weight presses down through every pipe in town. That's water pressure. No motor. No power. Just gravity.

But something had to lift that water up there. Electric pumps. And they stopped the second the lights did.

So the tower is now a battery... and it's draining. Many are built to hold about one day of normal use.

Day two. The level drops. The pressure drops with it. And low pressure is the real danger... because now dirty water can seep into the pipes. That's when a boil-water notice goes out.

So here's the move. The minute the power goes out, fill everything. Pots. Bottles. The bathtub. Ready.gov says one gallon per person, per day. The water is free today. Tomorrow it might not be there.
