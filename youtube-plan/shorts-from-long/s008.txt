Your gas station's tanks are full. The pumps are dead. Why can't you buy one drop? And why did one state write a law about it?

The gas sits in a tank underground. An electric pump lifts it. No power, no pump. The card reader's dead too.

So why not just keep a generator? After two brutal hurricane seasons, Florida passed a law. It doesn't make every station buy one. It makes many generator-ready.

Big stations near interstates, and every one built since two thousand six, must be wired so a generator plugs right in.

Only a few states, like Florida, Louisiana and New York, do this. But in a big storm, tanks run dry. During Irma, GasBuddy found fifty to seventy percent of stations empty in some Florida cities.

The gas you can count on is in your car. It doesn't need the grid. Ready.gov says half a tank, always. Full if evacuation looks likely.
